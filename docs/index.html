Built around the tapes on your cello

A beginner's fingerboard has coloured tapes where the fingers go. Ponticello's mark is exactly that board — the four first-position tapes at their true stopping distances, bunching as they climb — and so is the app: every note you are asked to play is drawn on your tapes, in your colours, so what you read on screen is what you see on the neck.

Everything a practice session needs

Three ways to read

Tab, score or highway — same music, your choice

Finger numbers on four string lines, engraved bass-clef notation with fingerings, or a Rocksmith-style highway falling or sliding towards the hit line. The fingerboard panel beside it lights the note you are playing, and can show the whole key to improvise in.

Make it your practice

Arranged down to where you are

Every song comes in four levels, from held bass notes a beginner can reach to the full melody. Pick the bars to loop, slow the tempo, choose which part of the original to play, and hear a backing track or the cello line while you practise.

Tuner

One pitch engine, one answer

The tuner uses the same analysis as the play screen, built for the cello's low register — so it never confuses a low C for the C an octave up, and it agrees with the practice feedback about every note.

Chords, worked out for the cello

Chord atlas

Shapes for C–G–D–A, not a guitar

Pick a tonic and a scale and see every chord in it — triads, sevenths and extensions — drawn on the cello's fingerboard in your tape colours, with roots as squares. Where no comfortable grip exists, Ponticello says so and gives you the arpeggio instead.

Chord charts

Lyrics and chords that scroll with you

Read a song as chord names over the lyrics or as cello shapes. The chart rolls along at your tempo, showing the current and next chords, and can preview the next fingering in grey on the one you are holding.

Progression builder

Write your own progressions

Start from a preset such as the pop axis or a jazz turnaround, or from nothing. Choose a key and a mode, tap chords into phrases, reorder them, and play the progression at your tempo. Drafts save themselves and export as JSON.

And the rest of the kit

What it will not do

  • No points, streak-shaming or leaderboards. Twenty minutes played badly is twenty minutes practised.
  • No account and no upload. The microphone is a tuner; nothing is recorded and nothing leaves the device.
  • No transposition behind your back. Arrangements move octaves, never keys, so the backing always agrees with you.

Free and open source

Ponticello is built with Expo and React Native, with a pure-TypeScript core — the fingering solver, the MIDI arranger, the pitch detector and the chord catalogue — covered by over 1,600 tests. Build your own library from your own MIDI files, fix a fingering, or add a feature.
